Land for Resorts and Hospitality Development in Kasauli: What Investors Should Know

Published 27 October 2026 · Madhur The Realtor

A 10 bigha non-TCP parcel in Kaba Kalan, next to Cassia Resorts — one of the development-scale parcels covered below.

Why developers are already looking at Kasauli

Two major hotel groups have already committed capital to Kasauli. IHCL, Taj's parent company, signed a 96-key Gateway resort in Kasauli with local partner Vasuki Hospitality Private Limited, part of a push that will take IHCL to 27 hotels in Himachal Pradesh, 12 of them currently under development. IHCL's announcement describes Kasauli as a sought-after leisure destination with colonial charm and strong proximity to major urban centres.

IHG signed InterContinental Kasauli the same year, through local partner So Hotels LLP, its first entry into Himachal Pradesh under the InterContinental brand. (source)

For a developer or resort operator, two signings from international hotel groups in the same small hill town is a signal worth noticing. It means the market has already been underwritten once, by companies with far more due diligence capacity than any single buyer. What it does not mean is that good land is easy to find. Most of what circulates informally in Kasauli is small, fragmented, or sits inside Town & Country Planning (TCP) or Municipal Committee (MC) limits, which restricts what can be built. Land outside those limits, in the size a resort or a housing project actually needs, moves quietly and does not sit on portals.

Being outside TCP and MC limits matters for a developer specifically because it changes what can legally be built and how. Land inside those limits is governed by municipal planning rules meant for town-scale residential and commercial construction, not resort or large hospitality layouts. Land outside them still requires the correct approvals for a hospitality or housing project, but starts from a cleaner position, without municipal zoning designed for a different kind of development layered on top.

Scale is the other constraint. A 96-key resort, or a housing project with internal roads and common areas, needs contiguous acreage, not a scattered set of small parcels bought from different owners over several years. That kind of land rarely surfaces through a general property portal. It moves through local relationships, which is also where ownership disputes and inflated asking prices get caught before a buyer wastes a trip.

The legal layer every developer needs to know

Under Section 118 of the Himachal Pradesh Tenancy and Land Reforms Act, 1972, a non-Himachali, or anyone who is not classified as an agriculturist under the Act, needs state government permission before buying land in Himachal Pradesh. This applies to corporate buyers and hotel groups the same way it applies to individuals. A company acquiring land for a resort still has to clear this permission before the transaction can proceed.

A bill to amend and simplify Section 118 was introduced in the Himachal Pradesh Vidhan Sabha, but as of December 2025 it had been referred to a Select Committee for review rather than passed, with the committee expected to report back in a later budget session. (source) The exact rules, especially around non-agricultural and commercial use cases like hospitality, are not finalised. Anyone evaluating a project here should confirm the current status directly, since this is an active legislative process, not settled law.

There is also a Kasauli-specific wrinkle worth knowing: because Kasauli is a cantonment area, some long-settled local residents are treated as non-Himachali for the purposes of this Act, in a way that would not apply in a non-cantonment town. This is the kind of detail that changes a deal timeline and should be confirmed with a local revenue advocate before any serious negotiation, not assumed from general reading.

None of this should discourage a serious buyer. IHCL and IHG both worked through the same framework. It does mean the permission process needs to be built into the project timeline from the start, not treated as a late-stage formality.

What real due diligence looks like before committing

For a resort or housing-scale purchase, the checks go further than a standard residential deal:

  1. Ownership verification. Confirm the seller actually holds clear title, and check for any pending disputes, before terms are discussed, not after.
  2. TCP and MC status, confirmed in writing. Don't rely on what a seller or local contact says verbally. Get the zoning status checked against the relevant authority's records.
  3. Section 118 eligibility, checked early. If the buying entity is not Himachali or not classified as an agriculturist, start the permission conversation with a local revenue advocate before negotiating price, not after.
  4. Site access and slope. A steep or poorly accessed parcel can look identical to a gentle, road-connected one in a listing description. This has to be seen in person.
  5. Water source and road access. Both affect construction cost and timeline significantly, and neither is always obvious from photos.
  6. Aggregation risk, if multiple parcels are involved. Buying several adjoining plots from different owners to reach the acreage a project needs introduces its own timeline and negotiation risk, and should be planned for upfront, not discovered mid-process.

This is also where working with someone who knows the specific belt pays off. A land parcel that looks right on paper can have a water dispute, an access issue, or a boundary question that only surfaces on a site visit with someone who has actually walked it.

Frequently asked questions

Can a company or hotel group outside Himachal Pradesh buy land in Kasauli?

Yes, but a non-Himachali buyer, including a corporate entity, needs state government permission under Section 118 of the HP Tenancy and Land Reforms Act, 1972, before the purchase can proceed.

Is Kasauli already home to major hotel brands?

Yes. IHCL (Taj's parent) has signed a 96-key Gateway resort in Kasauli, and IHG has signed InterContinental Kasauli, its first entry into Himachal Pradesh under that brand.

What does non-TCP, non-MC land mean?

It means the parcel sits outside Town & Country Planning and Municipal Committee limits, so it isn't governed by municipal zoning rules built for town-scale residential construction. It still requires the correct approvals for whatever is built, but starts from a different regulatory position than in-town land.

How large are development-scale land parcels available in Kasauli?

Currently available parcels suited to resort or housing development range from 10 to 42 bigha, though availability changes as parcels are verified and sold.

Is the Section 118 process the same for everyone?

The core permission requirement applies broadly, but local specifics vary, including a known wrinkle in Kasauli's cantonment area that affects how some long-settled residents are classified. This should always be confirmed with a local revenue advocate for the specific parcel in question.
